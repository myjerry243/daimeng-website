OUR COMPANY

Engineering for aluminium surface treatment

Foshan Diamond Technology Co., Ltd., known as Daimeng Technology, designs and manufactures aluminium surface treatment equipment in Foshan, Guangdong, China. Our work brings together production line engineering, automated handling and intelligent control for aluminium manufacturers. We focus on the relationship between the product being processed, the finish required and the conditions within the factory. By considering these factors together, we help customers develop equipment requirements that reflect their production goals and provide a clear foundation for technical discussions, project planning and implementation.

Our product range includes fully automatic vertical anodizing lines, horizontal anodizing lines, central control systems and related equipment. These solutions address different manufacturing requirements, from long aluminium extrusions to production programmes involving varied profile sizes and finishing routes. Rather than treating the production line as a collection of separate machines, our approach considers how loading, transfer, treatment, unloading and information management work together. This integrated perspective keeps the wider operation in view when individual equipment choices are made.

Solutions shaped around your production

Every aluminium finishing project begins with a different set of priorities. One manufacturer may be preparing for increased demand, while another needs to accommodate a broader product mix or improve coordination within an existing operation. A useful starting point is a detailed understanding of profile dimensions, alloy specifications, required finishes, output targets and working schedules. Factory drawings, available utilities and space constraints add the practical context needed to compare possible configurations and define a realistic project scope.

Vertical anodizing lines are an important part of Daimeng’s range. These systems use vertically suspended profiles and automated transfer between treatment stages. For manufacturers considering this configuration, the discussion extends beyond the line itself to building height, loading arrangements, maintenance access and the intended production mix. Horizontal anodizing lines provide another configuration for projects with different workpiece and process requirements. Selecting between the two calls for an engineering review of the complete production scenario, rather than a decision based on a single capacity figure.

Connecting equipment, process and control

Consistent production depends on the coordination of equipment throughout the process route. Daimeng’s central control systems bring handling and process functions into a common control architecture. The published system description includes both fixed and flexible production modes, allowing the operating concept to be discussed in relation to the customer’s product mix. Equipment movements, treatment requirements and production schedules need to be considered together so that automation supports the actual work performed on the factory floor.

Digital information is also part of the production environment. Daimeng’s control system supports reporting functions and interfaces for connection with factory management systems, including MES and ERP platforms. These connections require more than a network cable: both teams need to agree on data fields, record ownership, communication methods and how exceptions will be handled. Bringing production and IT specialists into the planning process helps establish a shared understanding of what information the line should receive, generate and make available.

Considering the complete finishing route

An anodizing project involves more than its main treatment stages. Loading and unloading arrangements, supporting process equipment and downstream finishing all influence the final configuration. Daimeng’s range includes related equipment such as automated loading systems, acid recovery equipment and electrophoretic coating equipment. Their suitability and integration should be reviewed against the selected process. Defining these requirements early helps customers and suppliers distinguish what belongs within the equipment package and what must be provided through other parts of the project.

Energy use, resource management and environmental considerations are part of Daimeng’s stated engineering priorities. In practical project discussions, these subjects need to be translated into specific requirements for power, water, heating, cooling, extraction and wastewater interfaces. The relevant operating conditions and responsibilities should be documented before the supply scope is finalized. Performance expectations are most useful when they are tied to an agreed production scenario, with clear assumptions and a suitable method of verification.

From a clear brief to a shared project plan

A well-prepared brief makes technical conversations more productive. We encourage customers to describe not only the products they manufacture today, but also the changes they expect the new equipment to accommodate. Representative drawings, finish requirements and production schedules help establish a common basis for discussion. Where information is still being developed, identifying those uncertainties is equally valuable. It allows the next stage of planning to focus on the questions that have the greatest influence on equipment selection and factory layout.

Clear project boundaries are another essential part of preparation. Equipment supply, building modifications, installation, commissioning, training and ongoing service should each have an agreed scope and responsible party. The same applies to documentation, maintenance access and the interfaces between suppliers. Discussing these matters alongside the technical configuration helps align expectations before implementation. Acceptance criteria should reflect representative products and operating conditions, giving everyone involved a practical basis for reviewing whether the agreed project requirements have been met.
